# Feel-Good Watchlist

Your taste, distilled: workplace or professional-world stories, fast hook (no slow burns), an outsider or veteran earning respect, contemporary setting (no period pieces), warm tone, no heavy/tragic themes, tends toward female leads. No Amy Schumer. Friend-trip movies (Girls Trip, 80 for Brady) and personal-life ensemble comedies (Crazy Stupid Love, Sisters) don't seem to land — the workplace/professional element is the key ingredient. Also loves the "unconventional approach proves skeptics wrong" story structure (Moneyball, Hidden Figures) independent of the field it's set in — not specifically sports (see the sports note below on Just Wright vs Ted Lasso). Dislikes plots built around a lie or fake identity/credentials (Second Act) — earning respect needs to come through genuine ability, not deception. Also not into catty/gold-digger rival-friend subplots (Just Wright). Note: the "unconventional approach wins" structure on its own isn't enough — Flamin' Hot has it and still didn't land, so it works best paired with a female lead and a contemporary setting (Moneyball is the exception, not the template). Strongest single pattern so far: an underestimated woman in a professional setting who wins on merit (Legally Blonde, Erin Brockovich, Hidden Figures, The Devil Wears Prada). Sequels to loved titles land too. Two refinements: the "no period pieces" rule isn't absolute — Hidden Figures is a period piece and is loved, so Maisel likely failed on something other than era. And action/espionage plotting is out regardless of cast (Spy), so the professional world should stay an ordinary one. Third refinement: a sports setting isn't a veto either — Ted Lasso is a 5x rewatch, so Just Wright almost certainly failed on the gold-digger rival subplot rather than the sport. Warmth and the workplace ensemble carry a sports backdrop fine. Fourth refinement: Chef landed, confirming that a male lead is fine when the story is professional reinvention winning on craft (Larry Crowne, The Intern, Moneyball) — the female-lead tendency is a lean, not a requirement; what's non-negotiable is the workplace, the merit, and the warm tone. Fifth refinement: 9 to 5 (1980) was rejected on sight as "too old" — so the vintage of the *film itself* matters even though the setting doesn't. Hidden Figures (2016) is a period piece and is loved, but it's a modern production; pre-1990 filmmaking reads as old regardless of how well the story fits. Keep suggestions to roughly 2000-onward productions; Baby Boom (1987) and Broadcast News (1987) are still on the To Try list but carry the same risk.

- [ ] The Hundred-Foot Journey (2014) — *an immigrant family opens a restaurant across the road from a Michelin-starred grande dame, and the son wins her over on cooking alone until she trains him herself — the Chef / Julie & Julia lane (professional world, respect earned on craft, a skeptic converted), warm, contemporary, no deception, no tragedy; small caveat: a brief arson incident and a fire in the family's backstory, both handled lightly*
- [ ] The Rewrite (2014) — *a washed-up screenwriter takes a teaching job he thinks is beneath him and wins the campus over by actually getting good at it — the Larry Crowne / Chef shape (career collapse, professional reinvention, respect earned on craft alone), ordinary workplace, contemporary, warm, no deception, no tragedy; small caveat: he sleeps with a student early on, played as his low point, not a romance*
- [ ] Baby Boom (1987) — *passed over for partner, builds her own company from a kitchen table and turns down the corporate buyout — the underestimated-woman-wins-on-merit spine of Legally Blonde / Erin Brockovich, ordinary business world, warm, no deception, no tragedy; Working Girl / Pretty Woman era*
- [ ] In Good Company (2004) — *a 51-year-old ad sales veteran is demoted under a 26-year-old boss and wins the office back on results and decency alone — the Intern dynamic with the ages flipped, ordinary corporate workplace, contemporary, warm, no deception, no tragedy*
- [ ] Broadcast News (1987) — *a network news producer who is flatly the most competent person in the building fights to keep her broadcast honest while the pretty anchor with no substance gets promoted past her — the Morning Glory / Late Night lane exactly (TV production workplace, a woman winning the room on sheer competence), workplace ensemble, warm and very funny, no deception premise, no tragedy, Working Girl era; small caveats: a short childhood prologue before the story jumps to present day, and the romance stays unresolved at the end*
- [ ] The High Note (2020) — *a superstar's overworked personal assistant is quietly a gifted record producer, and nobody in the building believes it until she puts a finished mix in front of them — the Devil Wears Prada assistant dynamic with the Late Night payoff (same director, Nisha Ganatra), plus the Begin Again lane of respect earned on craft; ordinary music-industry workplace, contemporary, warm, funny, no tragedy; small caveats: she goes behind her boss's back to prove the mix works (her own genuine work, not faked credentials), and a supporting character has hidden who his mother is, revealed late*
- [ ] Kinky Boots (2005) — *a man inherits his father's dying shoe factory and saves it by betting the whole floor on an idea everyone in the building thinks is ridiculous — sturdy high-heeled boots built for drag performers — with Lola, the outsider who pitched it, winning over the most hostile man on the line and the buyers in Milan on the quality of the work alone; the "unconventional approach proves skeptics wrong" spine of Moneyball / Hidden Figures set in an ordinary workplace, plus the Chef / Larry Crowne professional-reinvention shape, warm factory ensemble in the Superstore / Ted Lasso vein, very funny, contemporary, no deception, modern production; small caveats: it opens with the father's funeral (brief, the hook lands inside five minutes), Lola takes some homophobic hostility on the floor before the room comes around, and Charlie has an unsupportive fiancée pushing him to sell*

- [ ] East Side Sushi (2014) — *a single mother working her family's fruit cart takes a kitchen job at a Japanese restaurant, teaches herself to cut fish after hours, and is told flatly that a Latina woman will never be put behind the sushi bar — so she enters the competition and lets the plate argue for her; the underestimated-woman-wins-on-merit spine of Legally Blonde / Erin Brockovich / Hidden Figures set in the Chef / Julie & Julia kitchen lane (respect earned on craft alone, a skeptic converted), ordinary workplace, contemporary, warm, funny, female lead, no deception, no tragedy, modern production, hook inside ten minutes; small caveats: a brief non-violent robbery of the fruit cart is what pushes her to take the job, and it's a small indie — may need a digital rental rather than a subscription*
